# Creative catalogue: original top twenty editorial picks

**Status:** The following banks were materialized from the 10 October 2026 catalogue and are preserved here as usable text. They remain proposals. The catalogue's earlier statement that a second film was in progress and that zero twelve-style slots were qualified is a historical assembly snapshot; it is not today's completed-film ledger. This guide does not retroactively mark individual catalogue recipes rendered because a similarly named film exists.

These are the catalogue's historical editorial recommendations, not current owner-approved selections or guaranteed watchable stories. Reapply the latest comedy/suspense gate.

| Rank | Story | Title | Original reason | Proposed first version | Gate | Premise skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | EDU001 | The Half-Platform Problem | Equivalent fractions can be seen directly. A fixed reference rail makes the central comparison easy to inspect. | Two original platform tiles slide onto one shared unit rail. Use a brief prediction, the reveal and a clear final hold. | Check all lengths and subdivisions mathematically. Keep one reference whole throughout. | `story_premise_edu001_the_half_platform_problem` |
| 2 | ENT001 | The Runaway Finish Line | A moving finish line is immediately understandable and gives the character a clear change of strategy. | Articulated line runner, readable ribbon motion and three restrained camera setups. Finish on the quiet step through the anchored ribbon. | Check ribbon continuity, original character design and a safe cartoon tone. | `story_premise_ent001_the_runaway_finish_line` |
| 3 | EDU111 | The Gear Neighbors Turn Apart | Meshing neighbors supply a compact visible mechanism that is also useful for testing synchronized rotation. | Two large labeled gears first, then one additional gear. Use a clean diagram before considering a tactile 3D version. | Check tooth engagement, rotation direction and gear-ratio claims. A decorative mesh is insufficient. | `story_premise_edu111_the_gear_neighbors_turn_apart` |
| 4 | ENT025 | The Lantern Relay | The lantern gives a natural visual focus, a warm ending and a reusable handoff animation. | A short chain of characters passes one original lantern through a richly staged scene. Keep the handoff visible. | Preserve object identity, contacts and lighting. Use fictional safe props rather than fire-handling instruction. | `story_premise_ent025_the_lantern_relay` |
| 5 | EDU002 | The Basement Delivery | The mistaken count is small, concrete and can be demonstrated without a complex asset set. | An elevator travels on a clearly mathematical number line while a second counter exposes the off-by-one error. | Verify the signed arithmetic. Explain that floor labels are a model rather than a real building standard. | `story_premise_edu002_the_basement_delivery` |
| 6 | ENT008 | The Polite Bowling Ball | A polite bowling ball can show intention through timing and trajectory without elaborate dialogue. | Use one ball, a few original characters and a simple stage. Let hesitation and courteous detours carry the comedy. | Keep the physics visibly fictional where necessary and make the final objective readable. | `story_premise_ent008_the_polite_bowling_ball` |
| 7 | EDU021 | The Maze With a Waiting Line | A queue-based search can become a real visual trace rather than a vague coding analogy. | Use one fixed maze, a visible waiting queue and an identified search token. Reveal the visited frontier in order. | Check the algorithm trace, legal connections and stopping rule against a deterministic reference. | `story_premise_edu021_the_maze_with_a_waiting_line` |
| 8 | ENT031 | Across the Breakfast Canyon | An everyday tabletop seen at miniature scale gives rich spatial stakes with a small reusable prop set. | Build an original breakfast landscape. Establish scale, track the traveler and resolve the crossing with an earned choice. | Label the scene as fiction, check route continuity and avoid risky real-world imitation cues. | `story_premise_ent031_across_the_breakfast_canyon` |
| 9 | EDU027 | The Stripe Compressor Meets Confetti | Compressible stripes and noisy confetti make the difference in repeated structure visible. | Show the same encoding rule applied to two small patterns. Keep a compact symbol count beside the action. | State the exact simplified encoding. Verify counts and avoid claiming all real compressors behave identically. | `story_premise_edu027_the_stripe_compressor_meets_confetti` |
| 10 | ENT021 | Two Ends of the Map | Split map knowledge supports a clear cooperation story and a useful matched-view composition. | Start with each character holding one map portion. Reveal the combined route through shared action. | Keep the map geometry consistent and ensure cooperation, rather than a lucky coincidence, resolves the obstacle. | `story_premise_ent021_two_ends_of_the_map` |
| 11 | EDU052 | The Narrowest Gate | A narrow gate is a tangible way to introduce a bottleneck and show why local speed is not enough. | Animate tokens through three stations, change only the intended capacity, and show the resulting queue. | Define the toy process, rates and measurement interval. Do not generalize its result to every workplace. | `story_premise_edu052_the_narrowest_gate` |
| 12 | EDU081 | The Caption That Lost the Speaker | Speaker attribution in captions creates a practical accessibility lesson that can be shown directly. | Use two original voices or labeled characters. Compare ambiguous captions with a clearly attributed version. | Verify caption conventions and readable timing. Do not rely on color alone for speaker identity. | `story_premise_edu081_the_caption_that_lost_the_speaker` |
| 13 | ENT045 | The Block City Night Wave | A city-wide night wave offers a satisfying visual payoff and reusable timing controls. | A miniature block city passes one intentional evening signal from place to place. Let the camera reveal the larger pattern slowly. | Check the actual causal or fictional rule and avoid flashing lights or excessive brightness changes. | `story_premise_ent045_the_block_city_night_wave` |
| 14 | EDU004 | Same Fence, Different Garden | Equal fence length and different garden area create an inspectable geometric contrast. | Rearrange the same boundary pieces into two original plots, then tile their interiors using a shared unit. | Verify perimeter and area independently. Keep units and any grid scale explicit. | `story_premise_edu004_same_fence_different_garden` |
| 15 | ENT051 | The Three-Lamp Switch Dance | A finite lamp puzzle supports a real answer-checking fixture as well as an episode. | Show the legal switch action, allow a fair prediction, then reveal the verified sequence in a calm repeat. | Exhaustively verify reachable states and the claimed solution. Avoid misleading electrical-wiring realism. | `story_premise_ent051_the_three_lamp_switch_dance` |
| 16 | EDU063 | The Drawer That Counts Footprints | A drawer layout turns vague tidiness into a visible fit and access problem. | Use accurately sized original silhouettes on an overhead tray. Show why a visually compact layout may obstruct retrieval. | Check dimensions and the chosen access rule. Present one example, not a universal organizing prescription. | `story_premise_edu063_the_drawer_that_counts_footprints` |
| 17 | ENT060 | The Finite Spring Finale | A finite spring mechanism creates a built-in ending and a useful limit to the action. | Show the stored-motion setup, a short sequence of consequences and the calm state after the energy is spent. | Keep it a fictional prop. If making real mechanics claims, validate the energy accounting and safe boundaries. | `story_premise_ent060_the_finite_spring_finale` |
| 18 | EDU008 | The Giant Three-Point Jump | A truncated chart can produce a strong before/after reveal without a large scene. | Show the exact same three data points with two clearly labeled axis ranges, then inspect the apparent difference. | Use explicitly illustrative data, preserve the values and label axes. Do not manufacture an empirical claim. | `story_premise_edu008_the_giant_three_point_jump` |
| 19 | EDU013 | The Traveling Pulse | A traveling pulse can separate movement of a disturbance from movement of the material. | Use marked elements in a visible row. Track a few elements while the pulse travels through the group. | Check the model and boundary conditions. Do not generalize one wave type to all waves. | `story_premise_edu013_the_traveling_pulse` |
| 20 | EDU108 | The Flood Shortcut Stays Off the Map | A calm preparedness episode fits the survival/learning direction and avoids turning danger into a guessing game. | After source approval, use a clearly labeled map illustration to explain why the proposed flood shortcut is removed. | Hold production until current authoritative flood-safety guidance is checked for the intended region. Do not stage a traversable flood-depth challenge. | `story_premise_edu108_the_flood_shortcut_stays_off_the_map` |

These are menus, not obligations to execute every entry. `bank.json` holds the same rows as data.
