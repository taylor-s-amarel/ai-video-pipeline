# Run a bounded adoption experiment

Source: Animation and Video Production Master Guide (10 Oct 2026), "Bounded adoption experiments". The table is the owner's, verbatim.

These are proposed adoption experiments, not authorizations for new installations, paid compute, accounts, uploads, outreach or publication. Prefer already available tools. A new dependency should win a real comparison on quality, controllability, maintainability or accepted-output effort.

## The experiments

| Experiment | Intended outcome | Evidence needed | Stop or hold condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storyboard comparison | Select a premise with clearer suspense or comedy than the rejected story | Two or three genuinely different boards; explicit owner feedback or a clearly labeled review | No full render before stronger story evaluation; reject repetitive or unearned arcs |
| Existing stick rig polish | Better visible acting and prop continuity | Hardest-contact interval, three distinct poses, encoded motion review | Hold if the limb must stretch or a key action is unreadable |
| New Pymunk prop scene | One causal collision that improves the chosen story | Scene-specific geometry, stable bake, no-force control, negative tests and rendered-state match | Do not expand into body physics or scientific claims without separate work |
| Native versus layered depth | Choose the cheapest representation that preserves a needed reveal | Matched short shot, alternate-view or mask proof, review of exposed surfaces | Stop if 2.5D cannot show the required unseen surface honestly |
| Narration voice audition | Choose clearer, more natural speech for the actual tone | Representative clips, pronunciation check and real listening feedback | Technical synthesis success alone does not settle voice choice |
| Sketch route | See whether drawn reasoning improves a mechanism explanation | One short progressively drawn relationship with accurate underlying geometry | Reject jitter that changes factual dimensions or distracts from the action |
| Pixel or block style | Demonstrate a materially different visual construction | Original sprite/grid or true block scene, readable action, correct scale and export | A palette filter or title change is only a variant |
| One generative-video insert | Test a bounded atmospheric or transition use where exact identity is not essential | Exact model/version/rights, input/output, defect review and local resource evidence | Hold for access/license/hardware problems; do not use unvalidated generation for exact-count logic |
| Independent sound review | Establish speech intelligibility and score suitability | Listening of actual final audio, including names, transitions and payoff | Keep blocked if no listener or audio access is available |

## How to run one

1. Pick one experiment and write down its intended outcome, the evidence it needs and its stop or hold condition
   from the table before starting. One experiment at a time.
2. Check authorization first. The experiments are proposals: no new installation, paid compute, account, upload,
   outreach or publication without explicit approval. Prefer tools that are already available.
3. Build only the bounded proof the evidence column asks for (a matched short shot, representative clips, one
   collision, one insert), never a full film.
4. Compare against the current route. A new dependency should win a real comparison on quality, controllability,
   maintainability or accepted-output effort.
5. Apply the stop or hold condition literally. Record holds and blocks (access, licence, hardware, missing listener)
   as such; technical success alone does not settle a creative choice.
6. Report the result with its evidence status (proposed, invoked, focused tested, used in a film, ...) and what was
   not tested. Owner feedback, when given, is recorded as owner feedback and nothing more.

`experiments.json` holds the same rows for tools.
