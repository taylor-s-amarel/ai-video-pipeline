# Creative catalogue: sixty hook patterns

**Status:** The following banks were materialized from the 10 October 2026 catalogue and are preserved here as usable text. They remain proposals. The catalogue's earlier statement that a second film was in progress and that zero twelve-style slots were qualified is a historical assembly snapshot; it is not today's completed-film ledger. This guide does not retroactively mark individual catalogue recipes rendered because a similarly named film exists.

- Pick a hook for what it makes the viewer ask, then check its Guard against the chosen story. A hook whose guard fails for this story is not usable for it.

| ID | Pattern | Copy seed | Use | Guard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H01 | Visible contradiction | These two objects look the same. Watch where they behave differently. | Comparison | Use a real controlled difference; avoid unsupported universal claims. |
| H02 | Prediction pause | Before this moves, where do you think it will stop? | Prediction | Show all information needed for a fair prediction. |
| H03 | Hidden cause | The clue is in the part that barely moves. | Mechanism | The identified detail must actually explain the result. |
| H04 | Single change | I changed one thing. Follow what happens next. | Controlled test | Hold other relevant variables fixed or disclose them. |
| H05 | Result first | Here is the finished result. Let us rewind one decision. | Reconstruction | Do not conceal an essential step to create artificial mystery. |
| H06 | Scale surprise | Zoom out once, and this problem changes. | Scale | Label scale and preserve proportions. |
| H07 | Wrong turn | This looks like the obvious move. What does it miss? | Troubleshooting | Avoid demonstrating hazardous conduct as a usable shortcut. |
| H08 | Object viewpoint | If this little hinge could tell the story, it would start here. | Personification | Clearly separate narrative voice from factual explanation. |
| H09 | Missing piece | One piece is absent. Can you tell what it was doing? | Anatomy | The missing piece must be identifiable from visible evidence. |
| H10 | Count constraint | You get three moves. Which one would you spend first? | Puzzle | State legal moves and verify at least one valid solution. |
| H11 | Time reversal | The mess is already here. Which moment caused it? | Causality | Reverse presentation must not imply reversible real physics. |
| H12 | Small stakes story | All this character wants is a quiet cup of tea. | Fiction | Deliver a concrete conflict and payoff. |
| H13 | Alternative viewpoint | We have seen the traveler. Now watch the route. | Perspective | Keep identity and continuity clear. |
| H14 | Tradeoff | Make this faster, and watch what else changes. | Tradeoff | Name the conditions; do not invent numerical benefits. |
| H15 | Spatial question | Which side of this wall is actually doing the work? | Spatial mechanism | Use cutaways or labels so the answer can be checked. |
| H16 | Interrupted routine | Everything follows the pattern until this one arrives. | Pattern | The interruption must be visible and meaningful. |
| H17 | Quiet threat | Nothing has broken yet. Find the warning sign. | Safety observation | Use verified warning signs; no diagnostic certainty from a cartoon. |
| H18 | Limited resource | There is enough for one attempt. What should they try? | Dilemma | For real safety situations, replace guessing with approved guidance. |
| H19 | Delayed identity | You have seen its shadow. Now meet the object. | Reveal | Do not falsify a real object's silhouette. |
| H20 | Expectation swap | They brought a ladder. The problem needed a bridge. | Comic reversal | Keep the fictional solution coherent with the world. |
| H21 | One rule world | In this town, every door opens somewhere slightly wrong. | Worldbuilding | Establish the rule before exploiting an exception. |
| H22 | Tiny consequence | One dropped button starts a very long journey. | Chain reaction | Keep the chain causally legible. |
| H23 | Two clocks | These two timers disagree. Both are measuring something real. | Measurement | Define each quantity and unit. |
| H24 | Boundary case | This works until we reach the edge. | Limits | State the valid range and the boundary condition. |
| H25 | Constraint reveal | The difficult part is that this corner cannot move. | Engineering | Show the constraint explicitly and use a validated model. |
| H26 | Useful failure | Watch the attempt that tells us what to change. | Iteration | Show the observed failure without claiming unrun tests. |
| H27 | Common word clarified | We call both of these strong. They resist different things. | Vocabulary | Use sourced definitions rather than semantic tricks. |
| H28 | Invisible made visible | The motion is easy to see. Here is the force we are representing. | Model | Label arrows as representations, not direct measurements. |
| H29 | Comedic permission | The machine asked for one simple thing. The character overdelivered. | Fiction | The payoff should grow from the setup, not random spectacle. |
| H30 | Audience role | You are the lookout. What changes first? | Observation | Give enough stable viewing time and accessible cues. |
| H31 | Checkpoint question | At this point, what would tell us it is working? | How-to | The checkpoint needs a real observable criterion. |
| H32 | Order matters | These are the same steps in a different order. | Sequence | Verify that the claimed order effect holds. |
| H33 | Unexpected helper | The smallest character has the only useful tool. | Character story | Avoid a solution that appears without setup. |
| H34 | Trade places | What if the helper and the obstacle exchanged jobs? | Fiction counterfactual | Label imaginary rules; do not recast them as facts. |
| H35 | Map mismatch | The line looks shortest. The journey says otherwise. | Routes | Distinguish a fictional network from live travel information. |
| H36 | Layer peel | The finished image hides five separate decisions. | Production | Show actual source layers if claiming a real breakdown. |
| H37 | Sound clue | Listen once, then watch the movement that made it. | Audio mechanism | Use original or cleared audio and caption its key information. |
| H38 | Near miss | They almost succeed. Look at the final contact point. | Motion | Ensure failure is physically or narratively justified. |
| H39 | Empty space | The most important part of this design is the gap. | Design | Show the gap's purpose with a concrete comparison. |
| H40 | Wrong label | The label says finished. The motion tells another story. | Quality review | Use a fictional prop or verified defect; avoid deceptive claims. |
| H41 | Repetition with change | The first two attempts teach you the rhythm. The third changes it. | Comedy timing | The third beat must alter an expectation, not repeat a color change. |
| H42 | Perspective trap | From here it fits. Let us look from the side. | Geometry | Use consistent geometry across views. |
| H43 | Slow reveal | We will uncover one layer at a time. Keep your first guess. | Guessing | Avoid answer leaks in captions, thumbnails or audio. |
| H44 | Careful distinction | This picture shows a possibility, not a measurement. | Scientific model | Maintain that distinction throughout the episode. |
| H45 | Choice cost | They can save the object or keep their promise. Which matters now? | Moral fable | Show both costs without prescribing a universal moral. |
| H46 | Unknown setting | The character knows the rule. You do not, yet. | Mystery | Reveal enough evidence for a satisfying retrospective explanation. |
| H47 | Follow the token | Keep your eye on the marked piece. | Trace | Preserve object identity through occlusion and cuts. |
| H48 | Scale of effort | A tiny movement here produces a larger movement there. | Mechanism | Check geometry, leverage and any numerical claim. |
| H49 | Stop frame question | Freeze this moment. What is touching what? | Contact | Ensure visible contact agrees with the scene state. |
| H50 | Conflicting goals | One character wants silence. The other runs a bell shop. | Comedy | Give both characters understandable motives. |
| H51 | Model versus world | Our model leaves something out. Here is where it matters. | Scientific literacy | Specify the omitted effect and its consequences. |
| H52 | One object challenge | Tell a whole story with this umbrella. | Creative constraint | Use an original object design and genuinely changing action. |
| H53 | Repair evidence | Here is the symptom. Here is the check that narrows it down. | Troubleshooting | Do not leap from one symptom to a certain cause. |
| H54 | Hidden cooperation | It looks like a race until you see what they are carrying. | Story reveal | Plant evidence of cooperation before the reveal. |
| H55 | Two endings | The same setup can end in two different ways. | Branching | Label counterfactual branches and keep starting conditions consistent. |
| H56 | Rule inversion | For one scene, gravity has a different job. | Surreal animation | Make clear that this is a fictional physical rule. |
| H57 | Payoff promise | By the final frame, this shape will explain the whole scene. | Visual metaphor | Ensure the payoff actually resolves the opening promise. |
| H58 | Process clue | Do not watch the tool. Watch the mark it leaves. | Craft | Use genuine marks or clearly labeled illustrative animation. |
| H59 | Gentle reveal | The character thinks they are alone. Look at the background. | Warm fiction | Keep background evidence visible and avoid emotional manipulation. |
| H60 | Next useful question | We know what changed. Now what would we check next? | Learning | End with a bounded question rather than unsupported advice. |

These are menus, not obligations to execute every entry. `bank.json` holds the same rows as data.
