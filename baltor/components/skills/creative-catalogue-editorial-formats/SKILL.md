# Creative catalogue: twenty editorial formats

**Status:** The following banks were materialized from the 10 October 2026 catalogue and are preserved here as usable text. They remain proposals. The catalogue's earlier statement that a second film was in progress and that zero twelve-style slots were qualified is a historical assembly snapshot; it is not today's completed-film ledger. This guide does not retroactively mark individual catalogue recipes rendered because a similarly named film exists.

- The treatment groups use F01-F10 and F20. Original proposed durations are the catalogue's historical proposals, not exact requirements.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | One mechanism explained | A question, visible cause, resulting change, bounded takeaway. | Learning | Show a single causal chain and state model limits. | 45–75 seconds |
| F02 | Pause and predict | Setup, fair question, quiet thinking beat, result, explanation. | Learning | Do not use guessing to teach emergency response. | 40–70 seconds |
| F03 | Worked scenario | A concrete starting state, two or three decisions, checked outcome. | Learning | Verify every instructional step against the chosen source. | 60–90 seconds |
| F04 | Controlled contrast | Matched conditions, one stated difference, observed consequence. | Learning | Counterfactual outcomes remain hypotheses until validated. | 45–75 seconds |
| F05 | Trace the cause | Show an outcome, inspect clues backward, replay the verified chain. | Learning | Sequence must support causality rather than merely correlation. | 50–80 seconds |
| F06 | Character fable | A want, obstacle, choice, consequence and closing image. | Fiction | A lesson is a theme, not evidence for a general factual claim. | 45–90 seconds |
| F07 | Two-choice dilemma | The protagonist faces two costs and commits to one path. | Fiction | Both branches must be plausible within the established world. | 45–75 seconds |
| F08 | Dramatic irony | The viewer sees a clue the character misses until the payoff. | Fiction | Stage clues fairly and avoid a hidden last-second solution. | 40–75 seconds |
| F09 | Visual comedy chain | A simple task escalates through linked attempts and resolves. | Fiction | Every gag should follow from the previous action. | 30–65 seconds |

## Editorial treatment rules

- F01 explains the relationship through the story's visible action and bounded takeaway.
- F02 asks the story question before a fair thinking hold, then resolves it from visible evidence. It is excluded from the public-safety group.
- F03 separates one worked case into setup, action and a checked checkpoint.
- F04 compares initial and changed conditions while isolating one justified variable; no invented counterfactual result.
- F05 starts at the outcome, inspects clues, then replays the supported chain forward without overstating causality.
- F06 stages a want, obstacle, committed choice, consequence and the authored ending.
- F07 makes the costs of two plausible choices visible; an unexecuted alternate branch stays labeled proposed.
- F08 gives the viewer a fair advance clue before the protagonist notices it; no last-second coincidence.
- F09 lets readable actions and reactions carry the story with sparse captions and purposeful sound.
- F10 retells the same conflict from an existing prop/helper viewpoint without inventing a new solution.
- F20 alternates schematic and tangible representations of one relationship. Practical guidance needs an approved factual script first.

Learning combinations fail if their comparison has no controlled basis, their backward trace overstates causality, or factual verification removes the premise. Fiction combinations fail if their alternative choice, advance clue or object viewpoint requires a contrived new premise rather than illuminating the existing one. Every combination inherits the story risks, format gate, approach gate, rights review, caption readability, motion comfort, audio mix and exact-byte checks.

These are menus, not obligations to execute every entry. `bank.json` holds the same rows as data.
