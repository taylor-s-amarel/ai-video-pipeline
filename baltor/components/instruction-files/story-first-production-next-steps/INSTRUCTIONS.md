## Story-first production: next steps (owner's guide, 10 Oct 2026)

From the Animation and Video Production Master Guide (10 Oct 2026), "Practical next steps". These are the owner's stated priorities, verbatim; none is recorded as
done here.

1. Choose stronger funny or suspenseful storyboards with a real arc before another full render. Use the developed storyboards in this guide as proposed candidates; keep their unrendered and unvalidated status explicit.
2. Build one short proof of the hardest meaningful action, such as a fair misdirection, a visible handoff, a controlled chase or a planted reversal. A test clip is a development artifact, not a substitute for the eventual film.
3. Preserve the warm expressive stick strengths while testing one materially different style at a time. Use an existing renderer unless another route has a clear advantage.
4. Improve acting, staging and sound where they affect the story. Add layers only when they create depth, clarity, atmosphere or a specific payoff.
5. Complete the chosen film with actual sound, exact encoded review, source reconstruction and honest creative status. Use its failures to improve a reusable component or a future storyboard.
6. Refresh volatile tool/model licenses and access before a new install or distribution. Keep denied model downloads and blocked graphical capture visible rather than treating them as successes.
7. Recover the missing historical 460-effect atlas only if exact continuity matters to a future task. Do not delay current story work merely to enlarge a directory of possibilities.

The standard is a film someone has a reason to keep watching, followed by source that another creator can understand and reuse. More files, more layers, more candidates and more tests are useful only when they improve that result.
